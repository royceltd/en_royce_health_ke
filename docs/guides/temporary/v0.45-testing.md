# Outpatient Changes from the Bethesda Proposals — Testing Guide

This is a short testing script for version 0.45.0. It covers the outpatient changes asked for in the Bethesda proposals of 18 November:

- prescribing in dispensing units;
- ICD-11 diagnosis;
- the clerking order;
- vital signs history;
- the Visit Summary;
- request types and the Nursing Worklist;
- counter prescriptions.

Test on the demo, **hospital.royceltd.com**. Sign in with the role logins (Administrator for part A, then Reception, Nurse, Doctor, Cashier and Pharmacy), not only as Administrator. Tick each item as you go, and send a screenshot if anything is different.

## A. Setup first (Administrator)

The demo has no liquid medicine and no nursing procedure yet. Add one of each, as a hospital would for its own list.

1. **A suspension sold by the bottle.** Create an Item *Amoxicillin Suspension 125mg/5ml 100ml*: item group *Drug*, **Default Unit of Measure** *Bottle*, *Maintain Stock* ticked, with a cash price. Receive a few bottles into the pharmacy store.
2. Create its **Medication**: dosage form *Syrup*, linked to that item. Under **Dose and Dispensing Unit**, set:
   - **Dose Unit** *ml*;
   - **Pack Size** *100*;
   - **Default Route** *Oral*.
   - [ ] The form saves. The note under the section explains the pack size.
3. **A duration of 10 days.** If **Prescription Duration** *10 Day* doesn't exist, create it (number *10*, period *Day*).
4. **A nursing procedure.** Create a Clinical Procedure Template *IM Injection*: item group **Nursing Procedures** (the system made this group), billable, with a cash price.
5. Type *Request Category List* in the search bar and open it.
   - [ ] Five request types are there: *Laboratory*, *Imaging*, *Nursing*, *Procedures*, *Therapy and Counselling*. Each has its service point.
   - [ ] *Nursing* takes procedures in the *Nursing Procedures* group, and goes to **Nursing**.
6. On **Tablet Paracetamol 500mg**, tick **Prescription Only (POM)** (it isn't one, but it is the easiest to test with). Untick it again after part H.

## B. Vital signs history (Nurse, then Doctor)

Use a demo patient who has been seen before, or record vital signs twice for a new patient.

1. **Nurse:** record the vital signs for today's visit (give a temperature of 38.5) and submit.
2. **Doctor:** open the patient's consultation. In **Latest Vital Signs** at the top, click **History and trends**.
   - [ ] Every reading the patient has ever had is listed, newest first. Abnormal readings are in red.
   - [ ] Above the list, each sign (temperature, pulse, BP, SpO2, weight) shows its last value and a small trend line.
3. Close the box and use **View › Vital Signs History**.
   - [ ] The same box opens. It also works on a revisit with no reading today.

## C. The clerking order (Doctor)

1. On the consultation, open the **Observation** tab.
   - [ ] The fields come in this order:
     1. Chief Complaint, History of Presenting Illness, Review of Systems.
     2. Medical History (allergies, medication, past medical, past surgical, family and social history).
     3. OBS/Gynae (only for female patients).
     4. **Physical Examination** last, in its own section.
2. Fill them in and **Save**.
   - [ ] The past, family and social history you typed are saved back to the patient's record. Open the patient to check.

## D. ICD-11 diagnosis (Doctor)

1. **Diagnosis** tab › add a row. In the **Diagnosis** column, type *malaria*.
   - [ ] The list shows the hospital's own diagnoses first, then ICD-11 titles, each with its ICD-11 code.
2. Pick an ICD-11 title the hospital hasn't used before (for example *Malaria due to Plasmodium falciparum, unspecified*).
   - [ ] The **ICD-11 Code** column fills in by itself (*1F40.Z*).
3. Add a second row. This time type a code in the **ICD-11 Code** column (for example *CA40*, Pneumonia).
   - [ ] Codes starting with what you typed come first, with their titles. Picking one fills in the **Diagnosis** column.
4. **Save**.
   - [ ] Both rows keep the name and the code. Tick *Show more* or scroll down: the consultation's **Medical Codes** table lists both ICD-11 codes.
5. Type *Diagnosis List* in the search bar, and open the one just made.
   - [ ] It carries its ICD-11 code. The next doctor who picks it gets the same record, not a copy.

## E. Prescribing in dispensing units (Doctor, then Cashier and Pharmacy)

1. **Prescription** tab › add the *Amoxicillin Suspension* medication. Enter **Dose** *5*, **Frequency** *TID* (three times a day) and **Duration** *10 Day*.
   - [ ] **Dose Unit** fills in as *ml* and **Route** as *Oral*.
   - [ ] **Quantity** becomes **2**, and **Dispensing Unit** says *Bottle* (5 ml × 3 × 10 days = 150 ml, in 100 ml bottles).
   - [ ] **Start Date** is today's date. Give a **Start Time** too.
2. Change the duration to *5 Day*.
   - [ ] The quantity becomes **1** (75 ml fits in one bottle).
3. Add *Tablet Paracetamol 500mg*: dose *1*, *TID*, *5 Day*.
   - [ ] The quantity is **15** tablets.
4. Change the paracetamol quantity by hand to *10* and save.
   - [ ] The 10 stays: the doctor's number wins.
5. Complete the consultation, then **Cashier:** open the visit's bill.
   - [ ] The suspension is billed as **2 bottles** at the bottle price, not 150.
6. **Pharmacist:** open the patient on the **Pharmacy Worklist**.
   - [ ] The suspension shows 2 to dispense, with the instructions *5 ml TID for 10 Day, Oral*.
7. **Doctor:** print the prescription.
   - [ ] Each medicine shows its dose, route, start, duration and quantity.

## F. Request types (Doctor)

1. **Requests** tab. At the top, choose **Request Type** *Laboratory*.
   - [ ] Only the lab requests table shows; the procedures and therapies sections hide.
   - [ ] In the **Lab Test** column, each test shows the price for this patient. On an insured visit, it shows the scheme's price.
2. Choose **Imaging**.
   - [ ] Only imaging exams are offered, in the **Imaging** column.
3. Choose **Nursing**.
   - [ ] Only the procedures table shows, and it offers *IM Injection* but not *Appendicectomy*.
4. Choose **Procedures**.
   - [ ] *Appendicectomy* is offered, and *IM Injection* is not.
5. Clear the Request Type.
   - [ ] Every table shows again.

## G. The Nursing Worklist (Doctor, Cashier, Nurse)

1. **Doctor:** request *IM Injection* (Request Type *Nursing*) and a lab test, then **Send Requests**.
   - [ ] In "Where does the patient go next?", **Nursing** is offered alongside **Laboratory**. For a cash patient, **Cashier** comes first.
2. **Nurse:** Kenya Health › **Nursing Worklist**.
   - [ ] The injection is listed with the patient, the doctor and the time. While unpaid, it shows *Not paid: Cashier first*.
3. Press **Mark Done** before payment.
   - [ ] Refused: send the patient to the Cashier first.
4. **Cashier:** take payment. **Nurse:** press **Refresh**, then **Mark Done**, with the note *Given IM, left deltoid*.
   - [ ] It leaves **To Do** and shows under **Done**, with who, when and the note.
5. On the patient's visit, press **Status › Complete Visit** before the injection is done (repeat with a new patient).
   - [ ] Refused: *IM Injection: not done at Nursing yet*.
6. On the visit, open **Send To**.
   - [ ] **Nursing** is in the list of stations.

## H. Counter prescriptions (Pharmacist)

1. **Pharmacy Worklist › Counter Sale**. Walk-in name *Test Walk-in*. Add *Tablet Paracetamol 500mg* (marked prescription-only in part A) with **Frequency** *TID*, **Duration** *5 Day*, and leave **Quantity** empty. Press **Send Bill to Cashier**.
   - [ ] Refused: prescription only, enter who prescribed it.
2. Open **Prescription brought in**. Fill in:
   - **Prescriber** *Dr Jane Test*;
   - **Registration No.** *A1234*;
   - **Facility** *Test Clinic*;
   - **Prescription Date** today.
   Attach a photo of a script under **Photo of the Script**, then send again.
   - [ ] The bill goes to the Cashier with **15** tablets, worked out from TID × 5 days.
   - [ ] The label instructions read *TID for 5 Day*.
3. Open the bill (Sales Invoice).
   - [ ] The prescriber, registration number, facility, date and the photo are on it. The photo is attached to the bill.
4. Untick **Prescription Only** on paracetamol again (part A, step 6).

## I. Visit Summary (Doctor or Nurse)

1. On the visit from parts B to G, press **View › Visit Summary**.
   - [ ] One page opens with this visit's vital signs, then the clerking in the order of part C.
   - [ ] It shows the diagnoses with their ICD-11 codes, and the medicines with dose, route and quantity.
   - [ ] It ends with every request (where it went, and done, requested or cancelled), the lab results and the imaging reports.
2. On the patient's record, press **View › Patient File**.
   - [ ] Every earlier visit is there in the same layout, newest first. These are the previous visit summaries.
3. **Reception** or **Cashier:** open the same visit.
   - [ ] There is no **Visit Summary** button. If they open the print directly, it says the summary is for clinicians only.
